The Gap — July 19 → August 9

The Week — August 24–30

The lede. On August 24 the Supreme Court granted the government’s stay in No. 26A124 on the twenty-eighth day of silence, holding the States lacked standing because “Federal courts review final rules, not proposed rules—and certainly not antecedent internal directives to propose a rule”; the companion application, 26A139, was denied as moot A. The opinion is per curiam and states no vote tally. Two days later the Postal Service rule published, 91 FR 54966, effective August 21 — five days before its own publication A. In between, Judge Talwani found the government had violated her injunction by sending that rule to the Federal Register on the evening of August 21, imposed “no remedy other than identifying the violation,” and wrote that “The Supreme Court accepted the government’s feigned compliance” A. She then vacated her own injunction as compelled by the Supreme Court’s ruling, and on August 27 restrained seven parts of the published rule until September 10 A.

The week also ended this report’s longest-running absence. A Federal Register term search for the Epstein Files Transparency Act, which had returned zero documents for months, returned one on August 27 A. What published is a February 14 letter to Congress, republished six months later under a heading citing a different section of the statute, its six substantive pages carried as scanned images with no text layer.
