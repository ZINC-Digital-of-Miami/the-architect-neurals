## The Courts: Two Landmark Defeats Accepted, One Landmark Victory

- [ ] **Tariffs struck down, and the administration complied.** On February 20, 2026, in Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump, the Court held 6–3 (Roberts writing, joined by Gorsuch and Barrett and in part by Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson) that IEEPA does not authorize presidential tariffs — invalidating the signature economic program of the second term. The administration rescinded the tariffs by executive order and had already stipulated in January to refunds following a final decision; the Court of International Trade then directed Customs to process refunds of the roughly $166 billion collected. Whatever else is true of 2026, the president's flagship economic policy was struck down by the Supreme Court and he complied.
- [ ] **Birthright citizenship struck down.** On June 30, 2026, the Court ruled 6–3, again with Roberts writing, that the Constitution guarantees birthright citizenship to virtually all children born in the United States — 'Citizenship, then and now, was the right to have rights' — killing the day-one executive order. Trump acknowledged the loss and said he would 'work to correct it in Congress'; Speaker Johnson said Congress is weighing legislation and a constitutional amendment. Pursuing the goal through Article V and legislation, rather than defiance, is the constitutional route.
- [ ] **Removal power massively expanded.** One day earlier, on June 29, the Court in the Slaughter case overruled the 91-year-old Humphrey's Executor precedent, holding that presidents may fire the heads of independent agencies at will — with a carve-out for the Federal Reserve, where Governor Lisa Cook keeps her seat while contesting her removal. Trump celebrated it as 'the biggest and most consequential Decision issued by the Court, by far.' Scholars note this formalizes, as constitutional doctrine, much of the personnel control this document describes in Chapter 12.
- [ ] **Domestic troop deployments blocked — and ended.** In December 2025 the Supreme Court refused to allow the National Guard deployment into Chicago, after an Illinois district court found federal officials' factual claims 'not reliable' and flagged a 'troubling trend' of 'equating protests with riots.' On December 31, Trump announced the end of the Chicago, Portland, and Los Angeles deployments, and those federalized troops were withdrawn in January 2026. Guard contingents remain in Washington, D.C. and Memphis under different legal authorities.
- [ ] **Election administration.** In a 5–4 decision authored by Justice Barrett (joined by Roberts, Sotomayor, Kagan, and Jackson), the Court upheld a Mississippi law counting mail ballots received after Election Day — a ruling against the movement's ballot-restriction position, from a Trump appointee.
