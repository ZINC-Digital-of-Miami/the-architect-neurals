Germany / AfD

Named statements, court findings, campaign materials and documented international contacts.

Published weekly record through 2026-08-30 · Structured research reviewed 2026-09-08

Evidence & context

Documented record · Court finding

Landgericht Halle reports that Höcke's two convictions for using prohibited SA slogans became final after the Federal Court of Justice rejected both appeals in August 2025.

The cases concerned conduct in Merseburg in May 2021 and Gera in December 2023. They were prohibited-symbol convictions, not Holocaust-denial convictions.

Documented record · Court finding

LG Halle found that Björn Höcke knowingly used the prohibited SA slogan at the end of a campaign speech in Merseburg on 29 May 2021. Its 14 May 2024 judgment imposed 100 daily units of €130.

The court found deliberate public use and an effort to return the slogan to ordinary political language. The offense was use of prohibited symbols under § 86a StGB. The court’s September 2025 release reports the conviction as final; this is not a Holocaust-denial finding.

Documented record · Court finding

LG Halle found that Höcke deliberately prompted an audience in Gera to complete the prohibited SA slogan on 12 December 2023. Its 1 July 2024 judgment imposed 130 daily units of €130.

According to the court, he spoke the opening words and gestured for the audience’s completion while the earlier prosecution was pending. The completion was attributable to him. This final prohibited-symbol conviction must be distinguished from Holocaust denial or minimization.

Documented record · Court finding

On 15 October 2025, the Federal Court of Justice rejected Höcke’s hearing complaints in both SA-slogan cases, 3 StR 484/24 and 3 StR 519/24.

The court-authored texts are available through Open Legal Data mirrors. They follow the August 2025 rejection of the revisions. The legacy official document routes returned generic website content when checked; that access failure does not undermine the identified court texts or establish any further proceeding’s outcome.

Documented record · Court finding

VG Weimar rejected AfD Thüringen’s challenge to three passages in the 2021 state intelligence report. The court’s 28 August 2024 release says the agency could characterize a remembrance post by state spokesperson Höcke, which omitted Holocaust victims, as historical revisionism.

This is the court’s summary of oral reasons in 8 K 1272/23 We, an administrative reporting dispute. The release expressly said the judgment was not final and written reasons were not yet available. A later appellate disposition remains unresolved. The source identifies AfD Thüringen as the regional party organization and Höcke as its state spokesperson; it does not establish a criminal Holocaust-denial conviction.

Documented record · Attributed party statement

AfD’s 26 January 2020 remembrance statement identifies Jörg Meuthen and Tino Chrupalla as its federal spokespeople and attributes to them an acknowledgment of the Nazi murder of European Jews.

This dated party record establishes the named speakers and their published position. It is not evidence that every member shared that position or that these were their offices at all later dates.

Documented record · Attributed party statement

In a 9 March 2021 party statement, Meuthen and Chrupalla condemned Holocaust denial and minimization and said the federal board had initiated exclusion proceedings against a Bavarian member over a vaccine–Zyklon B comparison.

This is the party’s account of its stated position and steps it said it took. The statement does not establish completed expulsion, every member’s beliefs, or the party’s entire present position. It is relevant counterevidence to a blanket claim that the national party denies the Holocaust.

Documented record · Court finding

The Federal Administrative Court’s 20 May 2025 decision in 6 B 23.24 rejected the complaint against non-admission of revision concerning the federal AfD’s suspected-case classification. Its July release reported that the 13 May 2024 OVG Münster judgments had become final.

The decision addresses the grounds for admitting a further appeal. It is not a party ban, a criminal Holocaust-denial judgment, or a determination of every later intelligence classification or dispute. The operative date is May 2025; the publication release is dated 22 July 2025.

Documented record · Court finding

The European Court of Human Rights records that AfD member Rainer Edmund Maria Lanzerath was convicted in Augsburg on 23 August 2019 and fined €4,500 for two counts of Holocaust minimization under § 130(3) StGB.

His banner and earlier online image compared a yellow Star of David marked 1933–1945 with the AfD logo marked 2013–?. He argued that he was protesting marginalization of AfD members. The courts found the comparison blatantly minimized the Holocaust. The decision identifies individual membership and a congress setting; it does not establish party commissioning or adoption, or Holocaust denial.

Event or scheduled date: 2019-08-23 · Source review: 2026-09-08

  • European Court of Human Rights: Lanzerath v. Germany, application no. 1854/222022-07-28 · reviewed 2026-09-08Location: decision 2022-07-05; paragraphs 1-15; notified 2022-07-28
Documented record · Court finding

The European Court of Human Rights unanimously declared Lanzerath’s application 1854/22 inadmissible on 5 July 2022, with written notification on 28 July.

The four-page decision records the unsuccessful domestic appeals and the Federal Constitutional Court’s 21 September 2021 refusal to accept the constitutional complaint without reasons. It treats the conviction as a proportionate interference with expression. It does not attribute the member’s banner to the federal party.
