Historical comparisons

Original historical evidence, documented sequences, differences and counterexamples.

Published weekly record through 2026-08-30 · Structured research reviewed 2026-09-08

Evidence & context

Documented record · Historical reference

The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum records that President Paul von Hindenburg appointed Hitler chancellor on January 30, 1933; Hitler was not directly elected chancellor.

This historical sequence does not establish equivalence with a contemporary person, party or outcome.

Event or scheduled date: 1933-01-30 · Source review: 2026-09-08

  • United States Holocaust Memorial Museum: Hitler Comes to PowerPublication date not recorded · reviewed 2026-09-08
Documented record · Historical legal text and attributed museum context

The Enabling Act, proclaimed March 24, 1933, authorized the Reich government to enact laws outside the ordinary parliamentary procedure and permitted departures from the constitution within the act’s stated exceptions.

The translated law establishes the authority granted. The museum also documents intimidation and exclusion during passage. This historical sequence does not by itself establish a contemporary equivalent.

Event or scheduled date: 1933-03-24 · Source review: 2026-09-08

  • United States Holocaust Memorial Museum: The Enabling Act: translation of Reichsgesetzblatt I (1933), p. 1412026-04-03 · reviewed 2026-09-08Location: Translation, Articles 1–5; last-edited date describes the museum page, not the historical act.
Documented record · Constitutional text

The U.S. Constitution assigns legislative powers to Congress, provides a congressional bill-and-presentment procedure, and vests judicial power in a separate judiciary.

These are the written institutional rules in Articles I and III. Their existence alone does not demonstrate present compliance; that requires dated statutes, orders, judgments and implementation records.

Event or scheduled date: Not recorded · Source review: 2026-09-08

  • National Archives: The Constitution of the United States: A TranscriptionPublication date not recorded · reviewed 2026-09-08Location: Article I, sections 1 and 7; Article II, sections 2–3; Article III, sections 1–2.
Documented record · Official election results; correction C-017

The FEC’s official 2024 results record Trump with 77,302,580 votes (49.80%) and Harris with 75,017,613 (48.32%); the electoral-vote result was 312–226.

This corrects the preserved Chapter 10 statement that treats 2024 as a national popular-vote loss. Trump won a plurality; a share below 50% does not mean losing the popular vote. It does not predict the 2026 midterms.
